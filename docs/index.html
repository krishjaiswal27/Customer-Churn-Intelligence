the question

          
What the business needs to know.

        

      

      

        
More than one in four customers left: 1,869 of 7,032, taking about $139K of monthly recurring revenue with them.

        
The business needs three answers: which current customers are likely to leave next, what drives that risk, and what would keep them. This project answers each one. The overall churn rate is 26.6%, and every number on this page comes from the same cleaned dataset.

under the hood

          
The SQL, the PySpark port, and the audit that started it.

        

        
phases 1 to 3

      


      

        

          
          
          
        


        

          

            
A CTE with two window functions

            
Inside the CTE, AVG() OVER (PARTITION BY contract) gives every customer their contract's average monthly charge, and RANK() orders customers by price within their contract. The outer query then measures how far each customer sits above or below that average.

            
The features it builds on come from CASE WHEN rules in feature_engineering.sql, shown second.

          

          

            
            
          

        


        

          

            
Same logic, distributed

            
PostgreSQL runs on one machine. The PySpark version rewrites the same features with DataFrames so they would run across a cluster, then saves them as Parquet.

            
Both pipelines return 7,032 rows after cleaning and the same $66.40 average monthly charge for month-to-month customers.

TotalCharges was text, with 11 blanks

            
The first pass was pivot tables in Excel: churn by contract, tenure bucket and payment method. It also surfaced the dataset's one quality problem. TotalCharges was stored as text, and 11 rows were blank.

            
All 11 belonged to customers with zero tenure, who had not been billed yet. They were dropped, taking the dataset from 7,043 to 7,032 customers. The SQL and PySpark steps apply the same rule.

the model

          
XGBoost, tested on 1,407 customers it never saw.

        

        
XGBoost, scikit-learn

        
phase 4

        notebooks/ml_shap.ipynb ↗
      


      

        
The model catches 78% of the customers who go on to leave. About half of the customers it flags would have stayed, and that is a deliberate trade.

      


      

        
ROC-AUC
0.824
Ranks a random leaver above a random stayer 82% of the time.

        
recall
0.78
Leavers caught: 291 of 374.

        
precision
0.50
Flagged customers who really left: 291 of 585.

        
F1
0.61
Balance of recall and precision for leavers. Accuracy overall is 0.73.

      


      

        

        

        

          
Why precision is about 50%

          
Training used scale_pos_weight of about 2.76, the ratio of stayers to leavers. That makes a missed leaver cost more than a false alarm, so the model flags generously. The trade is deliberate: a missed leaver takes their monthly revenue with them, while a false alarm costs one retention offer.

          
The decision threshold can be moved to fit a retention budget: fewer, surer flags, or wider coverage.

          
200 trees, max depth 4, learning rate 0.1, 80/20 stratified split. The test set held 1,407 customers, 374 of whom left.

recommendations

          
What the business should do, and the evidence for each.

        

        
action

        
evidence

      

      
    
        
  • 
          
    Aim retention offers at month-to-month customers in their first 12 months.
    
          
    First-year customers churn at 47.7%, month-to-month customers at 42.7%.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          
    Move electronic-check payers to automatic payment.
    
          
    Electronic check churns at 45.3%; automatic card payment at 15.3%.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          
    Bundle online security and tech support as retention tools.
    
          
    Not having either pushes predictions toward churn; they rank fifth and sixth in the SHAP analysis.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          
    Put retention budget behind high-spend customers.
    
          
    Customers paying over $65 a month churn at 34.7%, against 10.9% under $35, and they pay the most each month.
    
        
    • 
        
  • 
          
    Offer discounted annual contracts to risky month-to-month customers.
    
          
    One-year contracts churn at 11.3%, two-year at 2.8%.

limitations and notes

          
What these results can and cannot claim.

        

      

      
    
        
  • 11 customers with a blank TotalCharges (zero tenure) were dropped, taking the data from 7,043 to 7,032 customers.
    • 
        
  • The dataset is a public IBM sample. The findings illustrate the method; they do not describe a real company.
    • 
        
  • Model metrics (ROC-AUC 0.824, recall 0.78, precision 0.50) come from the held-out 20% test set. The risk scores in the dashboard and the explorer come from scoring the full dataset, including rows the model trained on, so they demonstrate the workflow rather than out-of-sample performance.
    • 
        
  • Precision of about 50% is a deliberate trade for higher recall. The decision threshold could be tuned to a retention budget.
    • 
        
  • The chatbot is self-hosted and only live when its host machine is running.
